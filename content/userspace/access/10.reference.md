# Users and Access Reference

Every resource the gateway serves for users and access, every directive, setting and secret, and
every status a client meets, with a link to where each is shown at work.

## Resources

All of these exist in every application. `:identity` is an identity id. "Self" means credentials
of the identity in the path.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /identity/` | [Answers who the caller is](1.identities.md#sign-in); without credentials, creates a [guest](1.identities.md#guests). | anyone |
| `POST /identity/` | [Creates an identity](1.identities.md#an-identity-without-an-account) for new credentials. | no identity yet |
| `GET /identity/credentials/:identity/` | [Lists the ways to sign in](2.credentials.md#list-the-ways-to-sign-in). | self |

## Secrets

Values that must not be written into the Context. Each is given as a `$NAME` reference and
provided separately, as [Configuration](../platform/2.configuration.md) and
[Secrets](../shipping/6.secrets.md) describe.

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `identity.tokens.keys[].key` | Token key, generated with `toa key`. At least one is required. |
| `identity.basic.pepper` | Password hashing pepper. |
| `identity.federation.trust[].secret` | A provider's client secret. |
| `identity.federation.trust[].signature.key` | A provider's private key for client assertions, PKCS8 PEM. |

```yaml
# context.toa.yaml
configuration:
  identity.tokens:
    keys:
      - id: 2026q4
        key: $IDENTITY_TOKENS_KEY
  identity.basic:
    pepper: $IDENTITY_BASIC_PEPPER
    principal:
      authority: shop
      username: root
  identity.federation:
    trust:
      - iss: https://accounts.google.com
        aud: 1045282659797-example.apps.googleusercontent.com
        secret: $GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET
```

## Statuses

What a client of the application has to handle.

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `401 Unauthorized` | The request has no identity and the route needs one: no credentials, or credentials that are wrong, expired, revoked, banned, from another authority, or of an unknown scheme. Also a token of another application used outside its resource. | Sign in again. |
| `403 Forbidden` | The identity is known and not admitted: no directive admits it, a custom token's permissions exclude the request, the wrong scheme for an `auth:scheme` route, an unauthorized input property, credentials sent to an anonymous route, or a sign-up with credentials that already have an identity. | Do not retry as is. |
| `422 Unprocessable Entity` | Refused with a code: `INVALID_USERNAME`, `INVALID_PASSWORD`, `INVALID_CREDENTIALS`, `EXISTS`, `PRINCIPAL_LOCKED`, `INACCESSIBLE_SCOPE`, `UNKNOWN_CLIENT`, `FAILED`, `INVALID`, `MISS`, or a [provider code](4.federation.md#why-a-token-was-refused). | Read `code`. |
| `409 Conflict` | The username is taken. | Choose another. |
| `429 Too Many Requests` | Too many failed sign-ins from this address, or a throttled endpoint. | Wait `retry-after` seconds. |
| `451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons` | The request matched `censor`. | Nothing. |
| `400 Bad Request` | A malformed `host`, a missing required header, a body that is not an object where `auth:delegate` applies, or an OAuth error with `error` and `error_description`. | Fix the request. |

The body of a `401` or `403` does not say which reason applied, beyond the two texts quoted in
[Restrict Who Can Call a Route](6.access.md). The reason for rejected credentials is in the
gateway's log.

Alongside every successful status: **a response may carry an `authorization` header, and the client
must switch to the token in it.**

The reasoning behind identities, tokens and access on routes is in
[the model](../../model/edge/2.identity.md).
